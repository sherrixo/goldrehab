What does exercise actually do for weight loss? (The honest version)

By Sherri, Accredited Exercise Physiologist · September 2026 · 5 min read

"Eat less, move more" is four words that have done a lot of damage - mostly because they make a complex process sound like a willpower test. So what does exercise actually do for weight loss, per the research? The honest answer is more useful than the slogan.

What the research says

A major scientific review in Progress in Cardiovascular Diseases pulled together the evidence on exercise and weight. The finding that surprises people: exercise alone usually produces modest weight loss - a few kilos over months, not dramatic drops. But that’s not the headline.

The headline is everything else exercise does. It’s one of the strongest predictors of keeping weight off long-term - higher weekly amounts, roughly 150 to 250 minutes, are linked with preventing regain. And it improves blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar and cardiorespiratory fitness whether or not the scale moves at all.

From my practice

In 11 years, I’ve watched a pattern repeat: clients who chase the scale quit, and clients who chase strength and energy stay. The ones who stay are the ones whose bodies change - often with the scale barely moving, because muscle is going up as waist measurements come down.

The scale undersells the work. Every time. If you’re exercising consistently and your clothes fit differently, your resting heart rate is dropping, and stairs feel easier - that is progress, whatever the number says.

The takeaway

Exercise is a modest weight-loss tool but a world-class weight-maintenance and health tool. Train for what the scale can’t measure.

References

  1. Swift DL, Johannsen NM, Lavie CJ, Earnest CP, Church TS. The role of exercise and physical activity in weight loss and maintenance. Progress in Cardiovascular Diseases. 2014;56(4):441-447.

This article is general education, not medical advice, and it isn’t a substitute for seeing your GP, specialist or allied health team about your own situation. Everyone’s numbers - and the story behind them - are different.
