Can exercise actually prevent falls?

By Sherri, Accredited Exercise Physiologist · September 2026 · 5 min read

Here’s a number that surprises people: at least one in three Australians over 65 will have a fall this year. Falls are the leading cause of injury hospitalisation in older adults - and yet we talk about them like bad luck, like weather. The research tells a different story: falls are one of the most preventable health events there is.

What the research says

The Cochrane review on this is about as solid as evidence gets. In 2019, researchers pooled the trials on exercise for preventing falls in older people living at home. The result: exercise programs reduced the rate of falls by around 23% - and the programs built around balance and functional movements (standing, stepping, weight shifting) performed best of all. Fewer falls, and fewer people falling.

This is why the WHO’s physical activity guidelines specifically tell older adults to include strength and balance work in their week, not just walking. Walking is wonderful for your heart. But it’s balance training that teaches your body to catch itself.

From my practice

The biggest fall risk I see isn’t weak legs - it’s lost confidence. After a fall or a near-miss, people quietly start moving less, and everything that protects them (strength, reaction speed, balance) fades faster. Breaking that spiral is the real work.

In the studio it looks like: sit-to-stands from a chair, heel raises at the kitchen bench, heel-to-toe walking with a fingertip on the wall if needed. Little and often beats long and rare. And we look at the whole picture - vision checks, a medication review with the GP, loose rugs and poor lighting at home. Family members are always welcome in these sessions; two sets of eyes catch more than one.

If you’re worried about a parent or partner, or you’ve noticed yourself grabbing furniture "just in case" - that’s the moment to start, not to wait.

The takeaway

Falls are common, but they are not an inevitable part of getting older. Balance and strength training measurably cut the risk - and the best time to train your balance is before you need it.

References

  1. Sherrington C, Fairhall NJ, Wallbank GK, et al. Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2019;(1):CD012424.
  2. World Health Organization. WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. Geneva: WHO; 2020.

This article is general education, not medical advice, and it isn’t a substitute for seeing your GP, specialist or allied health team about your own situation. Everyone’s numbers - and the story behind them - are different.
