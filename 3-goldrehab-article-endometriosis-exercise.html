Does exercise make endometriosis better or worse?

By Sherri, Accredited Exercise Physiologist · September 2026 · 5 min read

This is one of the most common questions I get from clients with endometriosis, and it’s usually asked with real fear behind it: if I move, will I flare? It’s a fair question. When your body hurts for reasons you can’t always predict, exercise can feel like a gamble. Here’s what the evidence says.

What the research says

In 2025, researchers published a systematic review and meta-analysis of the trials on physical activity and exercise in women with endometriosis. Their conclusion: exercise was safe for most women, and was linked with reduced pain and better quality of life.

A dose of honesty: this research is still young. The studies are small, the programs vary, and endometriosis is different in every body. But the direction across the evidence is consistently reassuring - movement done well tends to help rather than harm.

From my practice

Endo-aware training, in my studio, means the plan flexes around your cycle, not the other way around. On flare days we swap intensity for gentle movement, mobility and breath work - because the pelvic floor often guards and tightens with chronic pain, and forcing through usually backfires. Between flares, we build strength patiently.

Your gynaecologist’s treatment plan always comes first. Exercise sits alongside it - and on the days it’s gentle, that still counts.

The takeaway

For most women with endometriosis, movement is safe and likely helps pain over time. The dose changes on flare days - the habit doesn’t have to stop.

References

  1. Xie M, Qing X, et al. The effectiveness and safety of physical activity and exercise on women with endometriosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. PLoS One. 2025;20(2):e0317820.

This article is general education, not medical advice, and it isn’t a substitute for seeing your GP, specialist or allied health team about your own situation. Everyone’s numbers - and the story behind them - are different.
